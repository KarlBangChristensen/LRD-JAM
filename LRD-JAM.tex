\section{Methods for Detecting Local Dependence}

This section summarizes the methodology for detecting local dependence (LD) in Rasch models
that is reviewed in \citet{johansson2026a}.

\paragraph{Residual correlations: Yen's $Q_3$.}
The most widely reported index of LD is Yen's $Q_3$ \citep{yen1984}, the Pearson correlation
between the standardized residuals of a pair of items after fitting the Rasch model. It is
produced automatically by the residual correlation matrices of most Rasch software
\citep[e.g.,][]{linacre2024,andrich2010,chalmers2012,robitzsch2024tam}, but its null distribution
is negatively centered rather than zero and depends on the number of items, the sample size, and
the number of response categories \citep{christensen2017}, so a single fixed critical value
(commonly between .1 and .7 in applied work) cannot be correct in general.

\paragraph{Partial gamma.}
For polytomous items, the analogue of the dichotomous Mantel--Haenszel test of conditional
independence is the partial gamma coefficient \citep{davis1967}, a pooled summary of the
Goodman--Kruskal gamma coefficients \citep{goodmankruskal1954} computed within each level of the
restscore formed by removing one item of the pair. Conditioning on a restscore that excludes one
item removes the part-whole bias that afflicts $Q_3$ \citep{rosenbaum1984}, and under uniform
(response-dependence-type) local dependence in the log-linear Rasch model
\citep{kelderman1984}, partial gamma consistently estimates the dependence parameter for
dichotomous items \citep{kreinerchristensen2004}. It is implemented in the \texttt{iarm}
\citep{muellersantiago2022} and \texttt{easyRasch2} \citep{johansson2026b} R packages and in the
DIGRAM software \citep{kreinernielsen2023}, and is ordinarily referred to its asymptotic standard
error \citep{goodmankruskal1954}, a reference that is suspected but not established to perform
poorly at the sample sizes and pooled tables met in practice \citep{vanderark2015,kreiner1987}.

\paragraph{Parametric bootstrap critical values.}
\citet{christensen2017} proposed simulating data from the Rasch model fitted to the observed
data, recomputing the residual correlation matrix for each simulated dataset, and using an
empirical percentile of the resulting distribution as a data-dependent critical value. Two
statistics were considered: the maximum residual correlation $Q_{3,max}$, and the maximum relative
to the average observed correlation, $Q_{3,*} = Q_{3,max} - \bar{Q}_3$ \citep{marais2013}, which is
more stable across scale lengths.

\paragraph{Rival indices and alternative tests.}
$Q_3$ has been compared against competing statistics repeatedly. \citet{kim2011} compared ten
indices of conditional dependence and found $Q_3$ among the best compromises of power and false
positives. \citet{houtsedwards2013} recommended a jackknife slope index together with a
likelihood ratio statistic for polytomous items in samples of a few hundred; \citet{edwards2018}
developed the jackknife index further but declined to attach a critical value to it.
\citet{finchjeffers2016} proposed a permutation test that randomly reorders the residuals of one
item in a pair. \citet{debelakkoller2020} proposed two nonparametric tests based on $Q_3$ that
require no estimated parameters, building on the marginal-sum-preserving resampling of
\citet{ponocny2001}; this resampling approach is established only for dichotomous items. Pairwise
likelihood ratio and Pearson statistics \citep{chenthissen1997} and a jackknife slope index
\citep{edwards2018} are further alternatives not reported by mainstream Rasch software.

\paragraph{Multiplicity correction across the item matrix.}
Every one of the statistics above is ordinarily evaluated per pair, but a scale with $k$ items
yields $k(k-1)/2$ pairs, so testing each at a fixed level makes a false flag somewhere in the
matrix nearly certain. \citet{debelakkoller2020} advise nominating only a few pairs in advance
rather than correcting across the whole matrix, reasoning that a Bonferroni-type correction
would drastically reduce power; this need not hold for every correction \citep{ferreira2024}. A
Westfall--Young step-down procedure \citep{westfallyoung1993}, which standardizes each pairwise
statistic against its own simulated null distribution and steps down using the joint bootstrap
distribution of the maximum, controls the familywise error rate and can be more powerful than
Bonferroni or Holm corrections when the pairwise statistics are dependent, as pairs sharing an
item necessarily are. The Benjamini--Hochberg false discovery rate procedure
\citep{benjaminihochberg1995} is a further alternative, though it scales poorly with the number
of pairwise comparisons in this setting.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
A positive conditional association under the Rasch model indicates response or trait dependence,
while a negative one indicates multidimensionality; the two mechanisms have opposite effects on
the unit of the scale and on reliability \citep{maraisandrich2008}. A single dependent pair can
also produce significant association for every other pair sharing an item with it while leaving
unrelated pairs unaffected \citep{kreinerchristensen2011}, so a rejected hypothesis is evidence
against the fitted model as a whole rather than against the one pair named in it
\citep{kreiner1987}.
